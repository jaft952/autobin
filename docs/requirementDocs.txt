1. Project Overview
================================================================================
1.1 Project Background

In indoor public spaces in Malaysia (shopping malls, universities, office buildings, etc.), the problem of ground litter accumulation around trash bins persists.
Small solid waste items (paper cups, plastic bottles, food packaging) frequently fall on the ground within a radius of 0.5–2 meters from trash bins due to:

Missed throws

Trash bin overflow during peak hours

Casual or irresponsible disposal behavior

Traditional scheduled cleaning systems (2+1 or 3+1 collection cycles) cannot respond in real time to waste accumulation, and manual cleaning incurs high labor costs.
In 2023, Malaysia invested RM1.9 billion in solid waste collection and public hygiene, yet waste management still faces significant challenges.

1.2 Project Objectives

To develop an autonomous intelligent waste management system consisting of two modules:

Module 1: Vision-Guided Aerial Trash Interception Robot

Module 2: Vision-Guided Ground Trash Collection Robotic Arm (Upgraded to 6 Degrees of Freedom)

The system enables continuous monitoring and automated cleaning of the area around trash bins, reducing human intervention and improving cleaning efficiency.

1.3 Application Scenarios

Indoor, flat, obstacle-free environments

Fixed areas around trash bins (0.5–2 m radius)

Normal indoor lighting conditions

High-traffic public facilities

2. Problem Statement
================================================================================
Problem 1: Ground Litter Accumulation and Limitations of Existing Automated Cleaning Solutions

Persistent accumulation of litter around trash bins in indoor public environments

Existing robotic vacuum cleaners can only handle fine particles and cannot collect three-dimensional solid objects (cups, bottles)

A specialized robotic system with manipulation capability is required

Problem 2: Low Efficiency and Rising Cost of Scheduled Manual Cleaning

Traditional 2+1 or 3+1 collection systems cannot dynamically respond to real-time waste accumulation

Waste accumulates rapidly during peak hours and remains until the next scheduled cleaning cycle

Manual spot cleaning is labor-intensive and expensive

Problem 3: Lack of Automated Localized Waste Collection Solutions for Fixed Trash Bin Areas

Existing automation solutions focus on large-scale logistics and centralized processing

The 1–2 m radius around trash bins still relies on manual cleaning

There is a lack of dedicated automated solutions designed for fixed-point waste collection

4. Functional Requirements
================================================================================
4.1 Module 1: Vision-Guided Aerial Trash Interception Robot (Catch Trash)
FR1.1 Airborne Object Detection

The system shall detect and track airborne objects within the camera field of view.

The system shall classify detected airborne objects by type and size.

The system shall achieve at least 80% confidence under normal indoor lighting conditions.

FR1.2 Trajectory Prediction

The system shall track airborne object trajectories in real time.

The system shall predict landing or interception points.

The system shall perform trajectory prediction within a defined time window.

The system shall update object tracking at a minimum defined frequency.

FR1.3 Navigation and Interception

The system shall navigate to the predicted interception position before the object lands.

The system shall prioritize objects with higher velocities.

The system shall dynamically adjust speed based on object distance and estimated impact time.

If interception becomes infeasible, the system shall slow down or stop.

FR1.4 Failure Handling

The system shall detect failed interception attempts.

After a failed interception, control shall be transferred to the ground collection module.

The system shall record interception attempts and outcomes for performance analysis.

FR1.5 Operational Constraints

Objects below a configurable confidence threshold shall be ignored.

The system shall operate only within predefined spatial boundaries.

Objects detected outside reachable interception zones shall be ignored.

The interception function shall be disabled under low-light conditions below a defined threshold.

4.2 Module 2: Vision-Guided Ground Trash Collection Robotic Arm (Grab Trash – Updated to 6 DOF)
FR2.1 Ground Trash Detection

The system shall detect objects on the floor within the camera field of view.

The system shall detect trash objects within a 0.5–2 m radius around the trash bin.

The system shall provide coordinates for each detected trash object.

The system shall classify detected ground objects by type and size.

The system shall achieve at least 80% detection accuracy under normal indoor lighting conditions.

FR2.2 Collection Priority

For newly detected objects, the system shall wait approximately 15 seconds before initiating pickup.

Pickup tasks shall be prioritized based on detection confidence.

FR2.3 Six Degrees of Freedom Robotic Arm Control

The system shall be equipped with a six degrees of freedom (6 DOF) robotic arm to enable flexible manipulation.

The 6 DOF configuration provides:

Three rotational joints for shoulder and elbow motion

Three rotational joints for wrist orientation adjustment

The system shall execute collision-free motion to reach the target object.

The system shall grasp the waste and transport it to the trash bin.

The 6 DOF design shall enable:

Multi-angle approach to objects

End-effector orientation adjustment for different object shapes

Operation in constrained spaces

Motion path optimization for higher efficiency

FR2.4 Performance Monitoring

The system shall record performance data including detection rate and pickup success rate.

The system shall classify operational failures (detection error, localization error, grasp failure).

The system shall export performance metrics for review.

4.3 Shared Functional Requirements
FR3.1 Mobility Control

The system shall independently control left and right wheel speeds to adjust interception paths.

The system shall avoid collisions while navigating to interception points.

The system shall limit maximum speed to ensure safe indoor operation.

FR3.2 Safety Functions

The system shall pause interception if a human is detected within a safety radius.

The system shall provide an emergency stop function.

The system shall immediately stop motion when obstacles are detected within a predefined distance.

5. Non-Functional Requirements
================================================================================
5.1 Performance Requirements
NFR1.1 Processing Speed

The system shall process visual input and update interception decisions within 200 ms per frame.

The system shall maintain at least 15 frames per second of effective tracking.

The system shall initiate motion within 100 ms after interception point prediction.

The system shall support real-time operation without cloud computing.

NFR1.2 6 DOF Robotic Arm Performance

The robotic arm shall complete motion from initial position to target position within 3 seconds.

Joint motion shall be smooth and free of vibration.

End-effector positioning accuracy shall be within ±2 cm.

The system shall support at least 3 pickup cycles per minute.

5.2 Accuracy and Reliability
NFR2.1 Prediction Accuracy

Under controlled indoor conditions, interception point prediction error shall not exceed ±30 cm.

The system shall correctly classify target waste objects with at least 80% confidence.

The system shall operate continuously under normal indoor lighting.

NFR2.2 6 DOF Robotic Arm Reliability

The robotic arm shall maintain stable performance during continuous 8-hour operation.

Grasp success rate shall exceed 75%.

The system shall detect joint limits and prevent over-extension.

5.3 Safety Requirements
NFR3.1 Speed Limitation

The system shall limit maximum linear velocity within safe indoor thresholds.

Maximum end-effector speed shall not exceed 0.5 m/s.

NFR3.2 Collision Avoidance

The system shall immediately stop motion when obstacles are detected within predefined distance.

The system shall not initiate interception when a human is detected within the safety radius.

5.4 Robustness and Fault Tolerance
NFR4.1 Error Recovery

The system shall tolerate temporary loss of visual tracking without system failure.

The system shall safely recover from failed interception attempts.

The system shall continue operating if non-critical components fail.

NFR4.2 6 DOF Robotic Arm Fault Tolerance

The robotic arm shall detect abnormal torque and trigger protective stop.

The system shall retry once after a grasp failure.

The system shall log all error conditions for diagnostics.

5.5 Power and Resource Constraints
NFR5.1 Battery Life

The system shall operate continuously for at least X minutes on a single battery charge.

Modules shall not exceed the power budget allocated by the system power unit.

NFR5.2 Computational Resources

CPU utilization shall remain below 80% during normal operation.

6 DOF inverse kinematics computation shall complete within 50 ms.

5.6 Modularity and Maintainability
NFR6.1 Independent Testing

Modules shall be independently testable without the ground collection module.

6 DOF robotic arm control shall be tested as an independent module.

NFR6.2 Configuration Flexibility

Parameters shall be adjustable without recompiling code.

Modules shall expose clear input/output interfaces for integration.

6 DOF joint limits and speed parameters shall be configurable.

5.7 Usability and Configuration
NFR7.1 User Configuration

The system shall allow configuration of interception sensitivity parameters.

The system shall provide visual or log-based feedback for debugging and evaluation.

The 6 DOF robotic arm shall support manual teaching mode for initial setup.

7. Interface Definitions
================================================================================
7.1 Vision → Decision Interface

Input: Object position (x, y, z), object type, confidence
Output: Decision commands

7.2 Decision → Mobile Base Interface

Input: Target position (x, y), speed
Output: Motor speed (left wheel, right wheel)

7.3 Decision → 6 DOF Robotic Arm Interface

Input: Target position (x, y, z), orientation (roll, pitch, yaw)
Output: Six joint angles (θ1, θ2, θ3, θ4, θ5, θ6)

7.4 Safety Monitoring Interface

Input: Sensor data, system status
Output: Emergency stop signals, warnings

7.5 Performance Monitoring Interface

Input: Operational events, timestamps
Output: Performance metrics, log data

11. System Uniqueness
================================================================================
11.1 Integrated Software System and Inter-Module Coordination

Unlike isolated solutions, this project implements a unified software architecture where a centralized coordination module explicitly manages resource arbitration and priority-based handover between Module 1 (interception) and Module 2 (collection).

11.2 Localized Fixed-Point Operation in High-Visibility Trash Bin Areas

The system is specifically designed to continuously operate within a fixed 1–2 m radius around trash bins — a high-impact area where cleanliness directly affects user experience but lacks automation solutions in current Malaysian infrastructure.

11.3 Embedded Real-Time Performance Monitoring

A comprehensive instrumentation framework embeds timestamps across more than 10 processing stages, enabling structured failure classification and automated latency analysis. This provides facility managers with quantitative deployment data for evidence-based decision-making.

11.4 Flexibility of 6 DOF Robotic Arm (Updated)

The 6 DOF robotic arm provides higher manipulation flexibility and broader workspace coverage than 2–3 DOF systems, enabling multi-angle approaches and handling complex spatial constraints.
